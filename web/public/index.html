How to read this

Demo data. The master is making up sounds at random spots and adding about ±3° of noise to each node's angle. The triangulation is the real code, but the confidence values are random (0.6–1.0) and mean nothing.

Node and its line
Each node has two microphones a few centimetres apart. A sound reaches one mic slightly before the other, and that time difference gives the angle the sound came from. The coloured line is drawn from the node along that angle.
θ (theta)
The angle the node reports, relative to the way it faces (the tick on the node). 0° is straight ahead; positive turns toward mic B, which is clockwise on this map. It covers −90° to +90° only: a two-mic node can't tell a sound in front from one behind it, so every node faces into the triangle and we assume the sound is in front.
Bearing
The same direction on the map: the node's facing minus θ, in degrees counter-clockwise from the +x axis.
Estimate
The point closest to all the lines at once (least squares). Lines from more confident nodes pull harder. It needs at least two lines.
Miss
How far each line passes from the estimate. If one node's miss is much bigger than the others', that node probably got the angle wrong.
Spread
The typical miss over all lines (confidence-weighted RMS). Small means the lines agree. With only two lines it is always zero, because two lines always cross, so it only means something with three.
Confidence
A number from 0 to 1 that each node's detector sends with its angle: how sure it is that it heard a real sound clearly. Its exact meaning is set by the DSP code (the protocol still marks it as proposed). Here it is used as each line's weight, and values under 0.5 are shown in red.
Heat map
Brighter means more of the lines point there. Each line is treated as a narrow wedge (±5° at confidence 1, wider when the node is less sure), so the bright patch shows how far the true spot could be from the estimate. A long, thin patch means the position is less certain along its length. Brightness is relative: the best spot is always brightest, even for a bad fix.
Quality
Good three lines, spread up to 5 cm. Fair spread 5–15 cm, or only two lines (no cross-check). Poor spread over 15 cm, or the lines cross behind a node (marked ? on the map: probably a front/back mirror). No fix fewer than two lines, or parallel lines. These limits are placeholders until we have real tap tests.
